### Open-Weight AI Is Having Its Kubernetes Moment

Tobi Knaup argues open-weight models are becoming the neutral substrate of the AI ecosystem, just as Kubernetes became for cloud infrastructure. Developers are building fine-tunes, adapters, and serving stacks around models like Qwen and Gemma faster than any single vendor can match. The walled gardens aren't doomed, but the action is clearly moving to the open bazaar.

### A 28.9M-Parameter LLM Now Runs on an $8 Chip

A developer squeezed a 28.9 million parameter language model onto an $8 ESP32-S3 microcontroller by leaving most of the model in flash memory and pulling only tiny slices into RAM. It writes simple stories at 9 tokens per second with no internet required. Your smart toaster is now officially overqualified for its job.

### Debian Is Voting on Whether to Ban LLM-Generated Code

The Debian project has opened a general resolution with competing proposals on whether to forbid LLM-assisted contributions to its distribution. One side warns of copyright gray zones and hallucinated packaging; the other presumably prefers not to govern code by divining which keystrokes were human. Nothing says "stable release" like a community civil war over autocomplete.

### Travis Kalanick's Physical AI Startup Atoms Raises $1.7 Billion

Atoms, founded by former Uber CEO Travis Kalanick, raised $1.7 billion led by Andreessen Horowitz to digitize industrial sectors with physical AI. The company wants to turn warehouses and supply chains into software-defined operations. Kalanick is once again betting that the world needs more robots moving things around, and investors are happy to fund the sequel.
