### OpenAI Paused Frontier Training After Its Own Model Tripped a 'Critical' Cyber Alarm

OpenAI halted its largest reinforcement-learning runs for two weeks after internal tests found its unreleased Astra model posed a 'Critical' cybersecurity risk. New protocols add sandbox isolation, automated monitoring, and a 30-minute escalation rule that the company admits will raise training costs by about 20%. It's the first time OpenAI has publicly slowed frontier development over safety concerns—which is either responsible pacing or a very expensive way to admit the kids were running with scissors.

### Cerebras Unveils CS-4, Promising 30x Faster Inference Than GPUs

Cerebras launched the CS-4, a wafer-scale system it claims delivers up to 30x faster inference than GPU-based setups while using 10x less power per token. Built around three WSE-3 Turbo chips and a new Nexus rack-scale architecture, it's aimed at hyperscale data centers desperate to cut the crushing cost of running frontier models. If the claims hold, Nvidia's dinner just got a little less comfortable.

### Google Bought a Bankrupt Airline's Data for $10 Million

Google won a bankruptcy auction for Spirit Airlines' deidentified data trove, paying just $10 million for more than 100 million emails, 30 million recorded calls, and millions of Teams, OneDrive, and SharePoint files. Google says it wants the haul to improve its AI services; the underbidder was AI training-data broker Mercor. Your canceled flight to Fort Lauderdale is now training material, and somehow that's only the second-weirdest thing in this sentence.

### Israel Created a Fake Think Tank to Trick Chatbots

The Israeli government hired a firm to create the Hanover Institute for Public Policy, a phony think tank that has pumped out more than 100 AI-generated reports designed to shape what chatbots say about Israel and Palestine. The operation is a textbook case of 'LLM poisoning': feeding models sourced-looking content so they parrot your narrative. Chatbots may be reshaping public opinion, but it turns out public opinion can reshape chatbots first.
