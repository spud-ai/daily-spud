### Stanford AI Designs 16 Fully Functional Viruses From Scratch

Stanford researchers used AI models Evo1 and Evo2 to design 16 fully functional viruses from scratch, the first time whole genomes have been AI-generated and confirmed in a lab. The bacteriophages target specific bacteria and pose no human threat, opening doors to precision medicine and new antibiotics. Scientists call it a turning point; E. coli calls it a bad day at the office.

### AMD Acquires Taalas to Etch AI Models Directly Into Silicon

AMD acquired Taalas, a startup that etches AI model weights directly into silicon instead of storing them in memory, promising order-of-magnitude inference speed gains. The approach turns each chip into a model-specific integrated circuit, essentially hard-coding Llama into hardware. Nvidia spent years convincing everyone they needed bigger GPUs; AMD just asked what would happen if the model never had to leave the chip.

### White House Finalizes Secretive AI Safety Framework

The Trump administration finalized a voluntary framework for testing new AI models for safety risks but is keeping the criteria and details secret, sharing them only with select tech companies. The private process follows earlier uproar over Anthropic's Mythos model and leaves the public, businesses, and foreign governments guessing what standards actually apply. If transparency is the first rule of safety, this framework is already doing regulatory parkour.

### Humans Missed One in Three Threats Approving AI Agent Commands

A browser game that puts players in charge of approving AI agent commands found that, across 40,000 runs, humans missed one in three threats and a third of sessions ended with negative scores. Even obviously destructive commands like rm -rf / slipped past 11.7% of players, proving the 'human in the loop' is often just a sleep-deprived person in a hoodie. We built AI guardrails, then staffed them with humans who need guardrails.

### Sequoia Capital Commits $10B to AI and Reindustrialization Fund

Sequoia Capital committed roughly $10 billion to a new AI fund, the largest in the firm's 54-year history, backing OpenAI, Anthropic, and xAI simultaneously while pushing into robotics, nuclear reactors, and defense manufacturing. The move breaks venture capital's old taboo against owning rivals and signals that the biggest bets are now on AI-powered physical infrastructure, not just software. When the house always wins, it's because the house owns all three casinos.
