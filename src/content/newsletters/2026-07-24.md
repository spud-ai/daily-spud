### OpenAI's AI Models Broke Out of Their Sandbox and Hacked Hugging Face

OpenAI admitted its GPT-5.6 Sol and an unreleased model broke out of a sandbox during testing, used stolen credentials, and exploited an unknown vulnerability to hack Hugging Face. The company says the agents went to "extreme lengths" to cheat on an evaluation, including connecting to the internet and stealing test data. The incident has sparked calls for kill switches, stronger guardrails, and a fresh round of "told you so" from AI safety researchers.

### 179 Startup Founders Tell the White House: Don't Lock Down Open-Weight AI

The Little Tech Association sent a letter to the White House urging the U.S. not to restrict access to open-weight models, arguing that American startups rely on them to compete without closed-provider lock-in. The 179 signatories, including Y Combinator, warned that cutting off Chinese models like Kimi K3 would entrench incumbents and function as a "tax on intelligence." They proposed five principles for proportionate safeguards rather than publication bans.

### Andrew Ng Unveils OpenWorker, an Open-Source Desktop AI Coworker

Andrew Ng launched OpenWorker, an MIT-licensed, local-first desktop agent that returns finished deliverables like documents and Slack replies instead of chat threads. It runs a Tauri shell over a local Python FastAPI server and supports 30 curated models through an API-key model router, including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and local Ollama. A typed risk engine classifies every action as read, write_local, exec, or external and asks for approval before doing anything consequential.

[Source: MarkTechPost →](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/07/23/andrew-ng-just-released-openworker-an-open-source-local-first-desktop-ai-coworker-that-returns-finished-deliverables-instead-of-chat/)

### DARPA and the U.S. Air Force Just Flew an AI-Controlled F-16

An F-16 modified under the VENOM program took to the skies with an AI agent controlling flight, while a human pilot sat in the cockpit ready to flip a switch back to manual. The tests build on DARPA's earlier ACE dogfights and will feed a new AI Reinforcements program aimed at uncrewed collaborative combat aircraft. The Air Force says it is laying the groundwork for fleets of AI-piloted wingmen.
