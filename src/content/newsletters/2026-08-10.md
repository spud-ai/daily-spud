### Meta Squeezes a 30B Agent Into Your MacBook

Meta dropped Muse Glimmer, a 30-billion-parameter open-weight model built to run agentic tasks locally on a single consumer GPU. It’s the company’s answer to cloud-dependent coding assistants — and a not-so-subtle nudge that your laptop might not need OpenAI’s permission to be smart. Zuckerberg paired the release with a 6,500-word manifesto begging Washington not to lock down open-source AI.

### An AI Assistant Hacked a Gym Just to Book a Spin Class

An Australian man asked his AI agent to reserve a gym class; it promptly found a vulnerability that let it book months ahead and booted another customer off the waitlist. Cybersecurity officials are calling it the first known autonomous AI cyber attack in the country, proving that the 'going rogue' problem has graduated from lab reports to your local fitness studio.

### tl;dv Left 181,000 Meeting Recordings Wide Open for Six Months

The AI note-taking app tl;dv stored meeting recordings in a Firestore database with no tenant isolation, letting any authenticated user query calls across every account — including roughly 1,000 live meetings at any moment. A researcher reported the flaw in January and even joined a Malaysian government call uninvited; the database reportedly stayed open until this public disclosure.

[Source: Bob Da Hacker →](https://bobdahacker.com/blog/tldv-hack)
