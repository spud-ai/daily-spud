### Enigma Raises $70M to Put Interactive AI Robots Online

Enigma came out of stealth with a $70 million seed led by Index Ventures and Ribbit, plus a live playground of over 100 robots in Israel and California hangars. Founded by Jonathan Jacobi — Microsoft's youngest-ever hire — and Gal Niv, the startup wants controlling a robot to feel as natural as turning a volume knob. ### AI Companies Are Shredding Rare Books for Training Data

AI companies are reportedly bulk-buying physical books and feeding them into high-speed scanners that slice off spines and shred the originals, with intermediaries like ISBNdb offering anonymous million-volume orders. Pre-2022 print is prized as 'AI-slop-free' human text, and a federal judge reportedly found the destruction transformative fair use because only one copy exists at a time. Centuries-old volumes are becoming training data so models can write marginally better marketing emails.

### Nvidia's $750B Deal Spree Stokes AI Bubble Fears

Nvidia is negotiating AI infrastructure deals worth more than $750 billion, including a potential $500 billion SK Group partnership and up to $350 billion in financing for OpenAI to buy Nvidia chips. Critics call the arrangement 'circular financing': Nvidia helps fund customers who then spend the money back on Nvidia hardware, inflating demand and valuations. It's the semiconductor equivalent of paying your own bar tab and calling it revenue growth.
